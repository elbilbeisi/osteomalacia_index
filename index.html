Interpretation: >35% osteomalacia · 15–35% bone biopsy indicated · <15% no osteomalacia (osteoporosis if collapsed vertebrae; neither if spine normal). The published Table 1 prints the second ALP band as “101–201”; 101–200 is used here. The algorithm reproduces all 41 published index scores in Table 3.

  • Derivation only. 41 retrospective patients (26 osteomalacia, 15 osteoporosis) chosen at the diagnostic extremes; the cut-offs and weights were fitted to this sample and never prospectively validated in the paper. Expect performance to be lower in unselected patients.
  • Reference standard. Osteomalacia was defined by osteoid volume >2.4% without tetracycline labelling — not the current mineralisation-lag-time definition. Some “osteomalacia” cases may have been high-turnover states.
  • Assay drift. ALP and 25(OH)D thresholds reflect 1980s assays. The 25(OH)D bands (<12.5 nmol/L) capture only profound deficiency by modern standards; current ALP methods have different reference ranges.
  • Not for CKD. All subjects had normal renal function; the Ca × P and ALP bands do not apply in CKD-MBD. Primary hyperparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism and Paget's disease were also excluded.
  • Collapsed vertebrae were present in 35% of the osteomalacia group, so vertebral fractures do not exclude osteomalacia.
